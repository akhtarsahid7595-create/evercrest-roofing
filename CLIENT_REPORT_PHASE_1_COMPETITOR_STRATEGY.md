Competitor research does not mean copying another company. It shows us what Google is currently rewarding in the same Dublin roofing market.

The Roofers Dublin research shows that the competitor has visibility for terms connected to:

- Roof repairs
- Emergency roof repairs
- Flat roof repairs
- Chimney repairs
- Gutter repairs and replacement
- VELUX and skylight repairs
- Roof replacement
- Roof slating
- Fascia and soffit work
- Storm-related repairs
- Roofing cost and informational questions

This gives Evercrest a clearer map of customer demand. We can then build better pages around services Evercrest genuinely provides, with clearer explanations, stronger proof, better internal linking, and more useful answers.

## What the research tells us about Roofers Dublin

The Semrush screenshots supplied for the Ireland database showed approximately:

- 55 organic keywords
- 178 estimated organic visits
- Authority Score of 17
- 481 referring domains
- 1.3K backlinks
- Approximately 11 organic pages
- 37 missing or untapped keywords in the comparison with Evercrest

The competitor is visible, but it is not impossible to challenge. A large share of the estimated traffic is concentrated on its homepage, while many service and informational terms rank outside the top positions.

This creates an opportunity for Evercrest to build a more balanced website where individual service pages can earn visibility instead of relying mainly on one homepage.

## Our strategy to beat the competitor

We will compete through quality, relevance, and trust—not through mass page creation.

### 1. Build only approved service pages

Evercrest confirmed that it offers the researched services. We will still create pages only when the service can be described accurately and supported with real business information.

### 2. Make each page more useful

New and improved pages will explain:

- The problems customers are experiencing
- What the service includes
- When repair may be suitable
- What customers should do next
- Common questions
- How to request an assessment or quote

### 3. Build supporting content carefully

Supporting “block” pages will answer genuine customer questions and link to commercial service pages. They will not be random blog posts or duplicated location pages.

### 4. Use real trust signals

Where available, we will use approved project examples, before-and-after images, genuine testimonials, accurate service areas, and consistent business information.

### 5. Strengthen internal links

Related pages will be connected naturally, for example:

- Emergency roof repairs → roof repairs
- Chimney repairs → roof repairs
- Gutter cleaning → gutter repairs
- VELUX repairs → roof leak guidance
- Supporting guides → the relevant commercial service

## How Google Search Console helps

Google Search Console is essential because it shows how Google sees the real Evercrest website. Semrush provides estimates about competitors; Search Console provides Evercrest's actual search data.

It helps us see:

- Which searches already show Evercrest in Google
- Which pages receive impressions
- Which pages receive clicks
- Average ranking position
- Click-through rate
- Search queries that Google associates with each page
- Whether new pages have been discovered and indexed
- Indexing or coverage problems
- Mobile and page-experience issues

This prevents us from guessing. For example, a keyword may appear as “missing” in Semrush but already receive impressions in Search Console. In that case, improving the existing page may be better than creating another page.

## What must be done manually after website changes

After each page is published, the following manual process is required:

1. Check the page on desktop and mobile.
2. Confirm the title, meta description, heading structure, links, phone number, and quote form.
3. Check that images load and have meaningful alt text.
4. Confirm the page is linked from relevant existing pages.
5. Submit the URL for indexing in Google Search Console.
6. Inspect the URL in Search Console for indexing or canonical problems.
7. Check the XML sitemap includes the page.
8. Test the quote form, phone link, email link, and any tracking events.
9. Record the publication date and URL in the SEO tracker.
10. Monitor impressions, clicks, queries, position, calls, and quote requests.
11. Review the page after Google has had time to recrawl it.
12. Improve the page based on real Search Console data rather than changing it every day.
